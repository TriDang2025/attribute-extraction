Differences from each source

What we changed or had to decide, why, and the likely effect on the results.

1. RSC (Latif et al., ICNLSP 2025)

What differsWhyLikely effect
No public code; rebuilt from the paper's Algorithm 1 (pre-split 15,000, recurse above 1,500, merge below 350, hard split above 2,500 chars, δ = 3).Only the paper text exists.Unknown fidelity in details the paper leaves out.
Embedding model = bge-m3.The paper does not name its model.Changes where breakpoints fall.
Start at LangChain's default 95th-percentile breakpoint; each recursion lowers it by 3 points; stop at the 50th.The paper says the threshold is 'gradually reduced' but gives no start or floor.A chunk still over 1,500 chars at the floor is left to the 2,500 hard split.
LangChain's sentence regex splits only after . ? ! followed by a space, and rejoins sentences with spaces.Used as published by LangChain.Turns without end punctuation merge with the next turn, so about half of RSC's cuts land inside a turn.
Final split: RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(2,500, overlap 0).The paper gives no overlap.Small.
Applied to short dialogues (about 1,000 chars).The paper's limits were tuned on news articles.In single dialogues the 1,500 / 2,500 limits rarely matter, so RSC mostly keeps one or two chunks.

Embeddings: bge-m3 instead of the OpenAI default.No API key, by choice.Different similarities; usually a strong open model, but not the published setup.
Scored with our turn-based metrics, not Chroma's token-IoU benchmark.Our data and question differ.Numbers are not comparable with the Chroma report.
